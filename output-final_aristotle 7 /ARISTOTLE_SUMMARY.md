# Summary of changes for run 6c258423-3eee-4739-97a3-4b10c45eaff1
Continued the work on the open problems from the attached paper (B. Grechuk, *A systematic approach to Diophantine equations: open problems*), adding two new Lean files. Everything builds with no `sorry` and no extra axioms (only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).
**New: `RequestProject/FunctionField.lean`.** A positive answer to Problems 2, 4, 6 or 7 for a given equation is most often obtained by exhibiting a *polynomial identity* — a parametric family `x(t), y(t), z(t)` of solutions. This file proves, unconditionally, that no such family exists for a long list of the paper's open equations.
* `constant_of_pow_mul_pow_eq`: over any field of characteristic zero, if `a·x^p·y^q = b·z^r + c` holds in `k[X]` with `a, b, c` nonzero constants, `p, q, r ≥ 2` and `x, y ≠ 0`, then `x`, `y`, `z` are all constant. The proof runs the Mason–Stothers theorem against the three-term relation, using a radical divisibility bound and the derivative branch.
* Corollaries for all four equations of **Table 13** (`x³y² = z³+6` — the smallest three-monomial equation of unknown solvability — `z⁴+2`, `z⁴−3`, and `x⁴y³ = z²+3`; Problem 6 open) and for the **Table 12** equations `x³y² = z⁴+1`, `z³+2`, `2z³+1`, `x⁴y³ = z²+1` (Problem 4 open). `cubeAddSix_eq_C` restates the flagship case over `ℚ` as "x, y, z are constant polynomials".
* For **Table 11** (Problems 2 and 7 open), using the reductions already in `Table11.lean` plus "coprime factors of an n-th power in `k[X]` are n-th powers up to a constant": `eqC_polynomial_constant`, `eqA_polynomial_constant`, `eq11b_polynomial_constant` show that every polynomial solution of `y(x³−z²) = z`, `y(x³−z²) = x`, `x²y²+x = z³` is constant apart from explicitly listed degenerate families (which are genuine and cannot be dropped).
**New: `RequestProject/FunctionFieldPowerful.lean`.** Handles right-hand sides that are not of the form `b z^r + c`.
* `natDegree_succ_le_radical_pair` (Mason–Stothers for a pair of shifts), `two_mul_natDegree_radical_le` (`x^p y^q` is powerful), `constant_of_powerful_eq_pow_add_const` (a general form subsuming the main theorem above), `constant_of_powerful_of_three_distinct_roots`, and `constant_of_pow_mul_pow_eq_separable_cubic`: over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, `x^p y^q = f(z)` with `p, q ≥ 2` and `f` a monic separable cubic forces `x`, `y`, `z` constant.
* `cubeSubXAddOne_constant` and `cubeAddXAddOne_constant`: the two remaining Table 12 equations `x³y² = z³ − z + 1` and `x³y² = z³ + z + 1` have no nonconstant polynomial solutions over `ℚ` (base change to `ℂ`, where the cubics split; separability certified by explicit Bézout coefficients).
These results exclude one very common route to a positive answer, but they do **not** settle any of the open problems themselves; the notes are honest about this. The method does not cover `y(x³−z²) = x+1`, the Table 5 equation `y²+x²y+z²x = 2`, or equation (19) `x⁴+y³+z²+1 = 0`.
